When anxiety rolls in, the hardest part is often just knowing where to start.
You may want to turn to Scripture.
But when your thoughts are spinning, picking a passage can feel like too much work all by itself.

That’s why it helps to keep a handful of verses ready — short, steady lines you can actually receive in those frazzled moments.
Not every Scripture speaks to anxiety in the same way.
Some quiet a racing mind.
Some remind you God is near.
Others give space to lay down what you’ve been carrying.

These are a few KJV verses many return to when worry weighs on them.

## 1. Philippians 4:6-7
> Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.
> And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.

This is one of the most familiar passages for restless, anxious thinking.
It doesn’t pretend worry isn’t real.
Instead, it gives you somewhere to take that worry:
- bring it before God
- pray honestly
- let his peace guard your heart and mind

This works well when anxiety lives mostly in your thoughts, when your mind keeps looping over the same concerns.

## 2. Isaiah 41:10
> Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.

This verse carries strength without sounding harsh.
It’s not just a simple “do not fear.”
It tells you the reason fear does not have the final word:
- God is present with you
- God will strengthen and help you
- God holds you fast

Many turn here when anxiety comes from uncertainty, heavy pressure, or fear that you cannot bear what lies ahead.

## 3. Psalm 56:3
> What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee.

It’s short, and that’s its power.
Long passages can be hard to hold onto when your mind is crowded.
This one is simple enough to repeat quietly to yourself.
It serves as a small anchor when you don’t have bandwidth for a longer reading.

## 4. Matthew 11:28-30
> Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.
> Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.
> For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.

Anxiety often comes wrapped in weariness.
This passage meets that weight with an invitation, not another requirement.
Jesus doesn’t start by asking more of you.
He offers rest first.

This fits well when anxiety mixes with emotional exhaustion, when it feels impossible to keep carrying all your burdens alone.

## 5. John 14:27
> Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.

Sometimes you don’t need deep explanations. You just need reassurance.
This verse frames peace as a gift given to you, not something you have to manufacture for yourself.
That matters a lot. Anxious minds often strain, trying hard to calm themselves down.
These words gently shift the focus away from your own efforts and onto Christ’s peace.

## Which of these verses will help you?
Each speaks to worry a little differently.
- **Philippians 4:6-7** for racing thoughts, when you need peace to settle your mind
- **Isaiah 41:10** for uncertainty, when you need strength and the promise of God’s presence
- **Psalm 56:3** for quick grounding, a brief anchor you can hold onto
- **Matthew 11:28-30** when anxiety feels tangled up with heavy fatigue
- **John 14:27** when you need quiet assurance of Christ’s peace

The aim is not to read page after page.
It’s to land somewhere your heart can actually receive, even when it feels fragile.
