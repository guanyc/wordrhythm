Nobody searches for verses about unity on a good day.
The searches come after something has already broken — a church that split, a family that stopped talking, a team where two people will not be in the same room.

There are two readers on this page, and they want different things.
One needs a line to send to someone tonight.
The other is walking into a meeting and needs to know what Scripture actually asks for, not just what sounds nice on a card.

These thirteen passages are for both of them.

## The verse everyone already knows

### Psalm 133:1

> Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!

Short, warm, and on roughly every church wall.
What people forget is what surrounds it.
The psalm goes on to describe oil poured out and dew falling — both of which run downward, not upward.
Unity in this psalm is something that gets poured, not something that gets decided.

That matters when you are the one doing the pouring.

## Unity is a command, not a mood

Four passages that treat unity as work. All of them are addressed to people who were not getting along.

### Ephesians 4:3

> Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

"Endeavouring" is the load-bearing word.
It assumes effort, and it assumes the unity already exists — you are keeping something, not inventing it.
The task is not to create oneness but to stop damaging it.

### Ephesians 4:2

> With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love;

This is the verse immediately before the last one, which is not a coincidence.
Paul lists the method first: lowliness, meekness, patience, forbearance.
Unity without those four turns into an argument about who is right.

### Philippians 2:2

> Fulfil ye my joy, that ye be likeminded, having the same love, being of one accord, of one mind.

"Likeminded" is often misread as "thinks the same things."
The verses that follow make it about posture rather than opinions — doing nothing through strife or vainglory, esteeming others better than yourself.

### 1 Corinthians 1:10

> Now I beseech you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment.

Paul wrote this to a church with named factions in it.
Note that he does not begin with the disagreement.
He begins with the divisions — the shape the arguing had taken.

## Scriptures on unity that name the hard part

The next four are the ones people skip, because they are less quotable and more useful.

### Amos 3:3

> Can two walk together, except they be agreed?

The bluntest verse in the list.
It is a question, not an accusation, and it is worth sitting with before a conversation you are dreading.
Agreement on the destination is a different thing from agreement on every step.

### Romans 14:13

> Let us not therefore judge one another any more: but judge this rather, that no man put a stumblingblock or an occasion to fall in his brother's way.

Paul moves the question from "who is right" to "what am I doing to them."
That is a much smaller question, and a much harder one.

### Galatians 3:28

> There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.

The dividing lines in that church were ethnic, economic and gendered — every one of them a real social wall at the time.
The verse does not say the differences vanish.
It says they stop being the thing that sorts people.

### Romans 15:7

> Wherefore receive ye one another, as Christ also received us to the glory of God.

"Receive" is the whole instruction.
Not tolerate, not forgive eventually, not wait for an apology — receive, on the same basis you were received.

## One body, so the pain is shared

### 1 Corinthians 12:26

> And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it.

This is the reason division hurts the way it does.
It is not a policy failure.
It is a body tearing.

### Ephesians 4:16

> From whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love.

Every joint supplies something.
If one part is treated as unnecessary, the whole body loses what that part carried — which is usually the argument for staying in the room rather than leaving it.

### Colossians 3:14

> And above all these things put on charity, which is the bond of perfectness.

Charity here is the fastener.
Not the feeling that makes unity pleasant, but the thing that holds the parts together when they would otherwise separate.

## What Jesus asked for

### John 17:21

> That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us: that the world may believe that thou hast sent me.

This is the prayer he prayed the night before he was arrested.
Whatever else is happening in your situation, unity is not a nice extra — it was on his list at the worst possible moment.

One honest caution before the list ends.
Unity in Scripture is not uniformity, and it is not the absence of conflict.
The New Testament is full of disagreements that were argued out in the open rather than smoothed over — Paul and Barnabas parted company over a personnel decision, and both kept working.
The goal was never a room where nobody differs.

## Which one to use

- **Sending one line to someone who is hurt:** Psalm 133:1 — it does not ask anything of them.
- **You are the one who has to go first:** Ephesians 4:2 — the method comes before the meeting.
- **A church or team with named factions:** 1 Corinthians 1:10 — it starts with the divisions, not the doctrine.
- **Before a conversation you are dreading:** Amos 3:3 — a question worth answering honestly.
- **You want to leave, and you are not sure you should:** Ephesians 4:16 — what your part supplies.
- **Praying rather than speaking:** John 17:21 — his words, not yours.
