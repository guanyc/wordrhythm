Gratitude is easy to feel for about ten seconds.
Then it turns back into a list: the meal, the drive, the message you still owe someone, the thing you are dreading on Monday.

A verse gives that feeling somewhere to go.
Not a long study — just one line, small enough to hold, that says thank you in words you would not have found yourself.

These are the KJV passages people reach for when they want to be thankful out loud.
Some of them are loud about it.
Others are quieter, which is often what a hard year needs.

## Thanks that comes first

Two psalms that put thanksgiving at the door, not at the end.

### Psalm 100:4

> Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.

This verse is written as movement.
You do not arrive thankful; you come in with thanksgiving, the way you would carry something in both hands.

It is a good verse for the beginning of a day rather than the end of one, when nothing has gone wrong yet and nothing has gone right either.

### Psalm 95:2

> Let us come before his presence with thanksgiving, and make a joyful noise unto him with psalms.

The same instinct, said louder.
"Make a joyful noise" is not a demand for a good singing voice.
It is permission to be unpolished — which matters if the only gratitude you can manage today is plain and a little clumsy.

## Thanks because God is good

The next two verses are almost the same sentence, said twice in Scripture, which is a fair sign that it was worth repeating.

### Psalm 107:1

> O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever.

### 1 Chronicles 16:34

> O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever.

Notice where the reason sits.
The verse does not say give thanks because the year went well.
It says give thanks because he is good, and because his mercy does not run out.

That ordering is the whole point on a day when you honestly cannot say the year went well.

### Psalm 103:2

> Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits:

This is the verse for forgetfulness.
It does not ask you to feel more grateful.
It asks you to remember, which is a smaller and more doable thing — one benefit at a time, named slowly.

## Thanks in ordinary things

Gratitude is not only for the big rescue.
Three verses put it in the middle of a normal Tuesday.

### Colossians 3:17

> And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him.

Thanksgiving here is attached to work, not to worship.
Whatever you are doing — answering email, driving, cooking — is the place the thanks is supposed to happen.

### Ephesians 5:20

> Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ;

"Always" and "all things" are the two words that make people nervous.
Read them as an aim rather than an exam.
The verse is describing a habit, not grading your worst afternoon.

### James 1:17

> Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.

This is the verse to read when you want to know where a good thing came from.
The giver does not shift or change his mind.
The gift was not a mood.

## Thanks when it costs something

The three hardest of the ten, and the three people quote most.

### 1 Thessalonians 5:18

> In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.

"In" every thing, not "for" every thing.
That one preposition carries most of the comfort in the verse.
It is not asking you to be thankful that something painful happened.
It is asking you to find something to be thankful for while you are still inside it.

### Philippians 4:6

> Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.

Thanksgiving is listed in the middle of a prayer about anxiety, which is unexpected.
The request and the thanks are supposed to travel together — you ask, and while you are still asking, you name what is already true.

### 2 Corinthians 9:15

> Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift.

"Unspeakable" here means too large to put into words, not forbidden.
It is the verse to end on when you have run out of specifics and still want to say something.

## Which one to pick

If you only want one verse today, choose by the shape of the day.

- **A normal morning, nothing wrong:** Psalm 100:4 — come in with thanks.
- **A year that has been heavy:** Psalm 107:1 — thanks grounded in God's goodness, not in the circumstances.
- **You keep forgetting:** Psalm 103:2 — name the benefits instead of chasing the feeling.
- **Anxious and thankful at the same time:** Philippians 4:6 — the two belong in the same prayer.
- **Standing in front of people at a table:** 2 Corinthians 9:15 — short, complete, and it does not require a speech.

## Watching for the season

The occasion chips are ordered by how soon they fall, so the list rearranges itself through the year.
Thanksgiving sits near the top for a few weeks and then drops below Christmas.
That ordering is a small thing, but it is the difference between remembering to send a verse and remembering too late.
